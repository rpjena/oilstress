# Monitor crowding, convexity and cash every day

A master fund that pairs a statistical-arbitrage equity book with a trend-following futures book faces two different failure mechanisms, and they meet in one place: the fund's cash. The stat-arb book does not usually fail through the beta or style exposures its risk model reports. It fails through a **hidden common factor created by crowding**. When the crowd deleverages, the average pairwise residual correlation moves from roughly zero to a few percent, and that alone multiplies book residual volatility by **3 to 7 times** (derivation below). This is why the August 2007 contrarian proxy printed an **−11σ day** and why the 2025–2026 "junk rallies" bled quant books in calm markets ([Khandani & Lo](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w14465/w14465.pdf); [MSCI](https://www.msci.com/research-and-insights/blog-post/unraveling-summer-2025s-quant-fund-wobble)). The trend book is long convexity at the horizon of its filter and short convexity below it. A reversal faster than the filter, as in March 2023, April 2025 or March 2026, hits fully built positions before the signals turn. The SG CTA Index lost **4% on 13 March 2023**, its worst day on record ([Reuters via Kitco](https://www.kitco.com/news/off-the-wire/2023-03-16/trend-following-hedge-funds-whiplashed-wild-bond-trade)). At fund level the books are close to uncorrelated in slow bear markets but can lose together in V-shaped policy reversals. They always compete for the same collateral: in March 2020, clearing members raised client initial margin by **55%** against a 32% rise at the CCPs themselves ([ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/pub/financial-stability/fsr/focus/2021/html/ecb.fsrbox202111_09~4f2668eed6.de.html)). Daily monitoring therefore has to go beyond VaR. It needs residual-comovement and eigenvector monitors, short-book squeeze metrics, reversal stress expressed in diversification-ratio units, vol-estimator overshoot, signal saturation, stressed margin, and diversification measured under stress correlation. Model health should rest on the bias statistic and the Acerbi–Szekely Z2 test, because Basel exception counts at T = 250 are nearly powerless. As of 12 November 2026 the stat-arb side is the live danger. Equity hedge funds are degrossing at the fastest pace in a decade, and the PivotalPath market-neutral index just had its worst October on record. The trend book's profits are concentrated in energy and rates while stocks and bonds are positively correlated. Nearly all alarm thresholds below are proposals to calibrate in-house, not published rules.

*How to read the evidence. A claim with a link is taken from the cited document. "Derivation" marks first-principles arithmetic that is reproducible but not claimed by any source. "Proposal" marks a practitioner design or threshold with no published hit rate. "Secondary" or "unverified" marks figures from aggregators, AI-assisted news rewrites or sources that conflict with each other.*

## Stat-arb risk is a factor the model cannot see until the crowd sells

### Crowding manufactures a common factor out of idiosyncratic bets

Pedersen's account of 2007 is the right physical picture. Crowding creates "a second, and endogenous, risk," and runs occur "only when the selling pressure is large relative to the available capital on the sideline." Three loops amplify an initial shock. In the **margin spiral**, higher volatility raises haircuts. In the **loss spiral**, losses and redemptions force sales. In the **risk-management spiral**, higher volatility tightens VaR limits. Pedersen's summary is that "a fear of forced selling leads to selling, and selling leads to forced selling" ([Pedersen 2009](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w15297/w15297.pdf)). Khandani and Lo's Unwind Hypothesis supplies the episode. Forced liquidation of one or more large market-neutral portfolios caused price impact that marked down similar books, which then deleveraged in turn. The authors call the evidence indirect ([Khandani & Lo](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w14465/w14465.pdf)).

The magnitudes show why. Khandani and Lo's contrarian strategy had a January–July 2007 daily σ of 0.36%. It returned **−1.47%, −2.88% and −3.99% on 7–9 August, then +6.85% on 10 August**. That is roughly −4σ, −8σ, −11σ and +19σ (derivation from their table) ([Khandani & Lo](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w14465/w14465.pdf)). Pedersen's quant long/short proxy, scaled to about 6% annual vol, lost about **25% in four days, which he calls more than 30 standard deviations**. Momentum and value became positively correlated during the unwind ([Pedersen 2009](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w15297/w15297.pdf)).

These sigmas are mostly a model failure, not a tail event. With equal weights over N names with residual variance s² and average pairwise residual correlation ρ̄, the book's residual variance per unit gross is s²[1/N + ρ̄(1 − 1/N)] (derivation). For N = 1,000 the book's residual σ is **3.2% of single-name s at ρ̄ = 0, 10.5% at ρ̄ = 0.01 and 22.6% at ρ̄ = 0.05**. A correlation shift too small to notice by eye therefore multiplies residual risk by 3.3× or 7×. Diversification is capped at √ρ̄·s however large N gets. The crowd's common position is the hidden factor. It is absent from a characteristic-based risk model because it is defined by who holds the stocks, not by what the stocks are.

### Measure the hidden factor directly: residual comovement and its eigenvectors

Two price-based measures can be computed daily. The first is Lou and Polk's comomentum. Take weekly returns that are industry-adjusted and residualized on FF3. For each stock in the winner or loser decile, compute its correlation with the rest of its decile over 52 weeks; CoMOM averages the two sides. High-CoMOM periods predict that momentum earns **about 1.06% per month less in year 1 (t = −2.72)**. They also predict crash days (momentum below −1%) in **22.5% versus 8.4% of days** (t = 6.24). The effect concentrates in names with high institutional ownership ([Lou & Polk](https://personal.lse.ac.uk/polk/research/hhab117.pdf)). The sign flips for value: high CoVAL predicts *higher* value returns, which the authors attribute to value being a stabilizing, negative-feedback strategy ([Lou & Polk](https://personal.lse.ac.uk/polk/research/hhab117.pdf)). A single crowding threshold applied to every factor is therefore not supported by the evidence. Positive-feedback factors (momentum, short interest, crowded growth) deserve tighter limits than value. MSCI's crowding model uses the same idea at a 63-day horizon: the average pairwise *specific-return* correlation inside each factor's top and bottom quintiles. In summer 2025 a **sharp fall** in this measure for momentum, liquidity and profitability marked a partial unwind in progress ([MSCI 2025](https://www.msci.com/research-and-insights/blog-post/unraveling-summer-2025s-quant-fund-wobble)). Both the level and the first difference are informative. A high level means fragility. A sudden drop means the crowd is leaving.

The second measure comes from random-matrix theory. The eigenvalues of a pure-noise residual correlation matrix fill the Marchenko–Pastur bulk, with upper edge λ₊ = (1 + √(N/T))². An unwind appears as an eigenvalue rising out of the bulk. Whether that eigenvalue matters depends on its alignment with the book's weights: a hidden factor orthogonal to the book is harmless, and one parallel to it *is* the crowd (proposal).

```
# Daily hidden-factor monitor (proposal; Lou-Polk / MSCI definitions adapted)
for f in {MOM, VAL, QUAL, LOWVOL, SIZE, SHORT_INT}:
    eps = risk-model residuals, 63d window, factor f itself excluded
    Co_f = 0.5*(mean_i corr(eps_i, mean_{j≠i} eps_j | top quintile f)
              + same for bottom quintile)
    z_f  = (Co_f - mean_3y) / std_3y ;  dCo_f = Co_f - Co_f[t-10]
E = residuals of book names, 60d;  C = RIE- or Ledoit-Wolf-cleaned corr(E)
lam_plus  = (1 + sqrt(N/T))**2
excess    = eigenvalues of C above lam_plus             # hidden factors
book_proj = Σ_k (v_kᵀw)² λ_k / (wᵀCw)                   # share of book resid var in them
align     = corr(v_1, w)
ATTENTION if z_f > 2 for a positive-feedback factor, or dCo_f sharply negative after a long rise
ACTION    if λ_1 > lam_plus and align > 0.3 and book_proj rising
```

The failure modes are known. No published daily threshold for any crowding indicator comes with validated hit rates. MSCI's composite weighting and the Goldman and Axioma crowding methods were not available. Holdings-based crowding data lags: MSCI's hedge-fund holdings are monthly with a **45-day lag** ([MSCI](https://www.msci.com/research-and-insights/blog-post/eyeing-the-crowds-from-multiple-perspectives)), and 13F is quarterly, so holdings data suits structural limits, not timing. Kritzman et al.'s absorption ratio, the share of variance in a fixed number of top eigenvectors, is the published ancestor of this monitor ([State Street](https://globalmarkets.statestreet.com/research/portal/insights/article/2a8e282a-343b-443c-b362-1634c71d2d26)). Applying it to *residuals* of an equity risk model is a practitioner extension with no tested early-warning record.

### Momentum is short a call on the market after bear markets

Momentum crashes are the archetypal factor tail, and they come in rebounds. Daniel and Moskowitz find that **14 of the 15 worst momentum months followed a negative two-year market return, and all occurred when the market rose**. In March–May 2009 the loser decile rose 163% while winners rose 8%; April 2009 WML was **−45.5%**. The mechanism is the loser leg's asymmetric beta, 1.56 in down markets and 2.16 in up markets, which makes short-losers a short call on the market. The paper's bear-market up-beta for WML appears as −1.51 in one place and −1.796 in another, so the source is internally inconsistent on that number ([Daniel & Moskowitz](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w20439/w20439.pdf)). Scaling exposure by forecast variance is the robust fix. Barroso and Santa-Clara's inverse-vol momentum raises the Sharpe ratio from 0.53 to 0.97 and improves the **worst month from −80.0% to −28.4%** ([CXO summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/momentum-investing/avoiding-momentum-strategy-crashes/)).

Daily data repeat the pattern. On the Pfizer vaccine day, 9 November 2020, momentum fell **7–10σ** against Axioma's predicted vol in most regions, and **9 of 12 US factors moved more than 2σ** ([Qontigo/Axioma](https://qontigo.com/pfizer-vaccine-announcement-puts-momentum-in-the-icu/)). Goldman flagged a **>4σ momentum drawdown on 7 and 10 March 2025** that forced derisking across strategies ([GS Marquee](https://marquee.gs.com/welcome/news/views-from-the-trading-floor/2025-in-review)). In July 2026 the S&P long/short momentum index fell more than 3% in each of two consecutive weeks, its worst two weeks in over three years, after reaching a record high ([Hedgeweek, 7 Jul 2026](https://www.hedgeweek.com/quant-funds-suffer-steepest-momentum-drawdown-since-2023-amid-factor-rotation/)). The count of factors moving more than 2σ in a day is a cheap regime-break detector. Momentum's daily z-score against its ex-ante σ is the specific crash monitor.

```
# Momentum crash state (proposal implementing Daniel-Moskowitz conditioning)
bear  = cum_mkt_return(24m) < 0
hivar = var_mkt(126d) > 80th historical percentile
breadth = #{factors with |r_k/σ_k| > 2} today
if bear and hivar: halve book momentum and loser-reversal exposure, or buy upside market convexity
if breadth >= 0.5*K or |z_MOM| > 3: intraday factor review
```

### Short books carry convex squeeze risk that linear models book as alpha

The short leg has unbounded convex losses and a funding channel the long leg lacks: recall risk and borrow-fee spikes. Summer 2025 showed the subtler problem. From 1 June to 25 July 2025, Goldman Prime estimated quant equity managers lost **4.2% while US equities rose about 8%**. Factor returns were about **twice their forecast**. MSCI found that **84% of the most-shorted names** carried at least one "junk" exposure (high residual vol, liquidity, beta, small size or low profitability), and that their *specific* returns rose steadily ([MSCI 2025](https://www.msci.com/research-and-insights/blog-post/unraveling-summer-2025s-quant-fund-wobble)). A linear factor model files this interaction as idiosyncratic noise, so a book can look factor-neutral and still lose heavily. Goldman explicitly linked the January 2026 losses (UBS estimated **−2.8%** for US quants in two weeks) to "adverse idiosyncratic moves mainly in the short book, similar to the June–July drawdown" ([Investing.com, AI-assisted](https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/quant-hedge-funds-start-2026-in-the-red-as-crowded-trades-falter-93CH-4458625)). In January 2021, Acadian estimated that a portfolio of the most popular longs and shorts lost more than 30% in the month. Fifty-nine Russell 3000 names traded at more than 3× six-month volume, while stock correlations sat near their lowest level since 2019 ([Advisor Perspectives/Bloomberg](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/articles/2021/02/03/hedge-funds-live-to-fight-another-day-after-reddit-mayhem)). Squeezes happen when index correlation is low, so index-vol indicators miss them.

The daily short-book metrics are all proposals. The first is short-crowding exposure, Σ|wᵢ|·SIᵢ/floatᵢ, together with the share of short gross with days-to-cover above 5 or SI/float above 20%. The second is lending stress: gross-weighted utilization, the share of shorts with fees above about 5% p.a., and day-on-day changes, since utilization above roughly 90% tends to precede recalls. The third is P&L bucketed on two-dimensional quantiles such as short interest × residual vol and short interest × beta. Persistent negative specific P&L in the top short-interest bucket is factor risk the model misses. The fourth is a squeeze stress, rᵢ = +k·σᵢ scaled by SI percentile, applied to the MSCI junk-interaction basket. The fifth is attention proxies: volume/ADV above 3 and short-dated call volume on shorted names. Event risk belongs here too. Diffusion σ understates jumps, so earnings exposure should be measured as event-VaR = √(Σ wᵢ² mᵢ²), with mᵢ the option-implied earnings move, and capped as a fraction of diffusion VaR (proposal). Shorts in plausible takeover targets carry jump risk that diffusion VaR ignores. In 2007 the collapse of the LBO premium acted as a common factor against value ([Pedersen 2009](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w15297/w15297.pdf)).

### Liquidity cost scales as Q^{3/2}, and the crowd's Q is the one that matters

The square-root law says a metaorder of size Q moves price by I ≈ Y·σ_d·√(Q/V), roughly independent of how it is sliced, as long as participation is not too high ([Bouchaud](https://bouchaud.substack.com/p/the-square-root-law-of-market-impact)). Its microfoundation is a latent order book whose density vanishes linearly near the price ([Tóth et al.](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/1105.1694v3)). The prefactor Y "of order 1" comes from the literature and was not re-verified here. Two consequences follow (derivation). First, dollar cost is Q·I ∝ Q^{3/2}, so **doubling gross raises liquidation cost 2.8× and cost per unit of gross by √2**. Second, when a crowd holding Q_c exits alongside you, your impact becomes Y·σ·√((Q + Q_c)/V), which multiplies your cost by √(1 + Q_c/Q). Liquidity also deteriorates before the crash. Khandani and Lo's Kyle-lambda measure **rose 1.5× on 26 July 2007**, and weekly volume ran at 2.9–3.6bn shares against roughly 1.9bn normal for four weeks ([Khandani & Lo](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w14465/w14465.pdf)). The in-house analogue is realized implementation shortfall divided by the pre-trade model. A five-day ratio above about 1.5 is the proposed alert.

```
# Crowded liquidity-adjusted VaR (proposal)
DTL_i   = |q_i| / (p * ADV_i),  p = 10-20%        # report % of gross liquid in 1/3/5/10d, long & short separately
H       = max_i DTL_i
cost_i  = (0.5*spread_i + Y*σ_i^stress*sqrt(|q_i|/V_i^stress)) * |q_i| * sqrt(1 + Qcrowd_i/|q_i|)
          with V^stress = 0.5*ADV, σ^stress = 2σ, Qcrowd from SI (shorts) or PB/13F overlap (longs)
LVaR    = VaR_FHS(horizon ≈ H/3 for linear unwind) + Σ_i cost_i
```

The H/3 horizon reflects the variance of a linearly decaying position, ∫₀^τ(1 − t/τ)²σ²dt = σ²τ/3 (derivation). Leverage converts these costs into forced selling. Khandani and Lo cite 4:1 to 10:1 gross-to-equity as common for quant market-neutral managers in 2007 ([Khandani & Lo](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w14465/w14465.pdf)). A secondary blog relays a JPMorgan estimate that quant equity gross stood near **450% in July 2026, the highest of any strategy** ([algotrader.ch, secondary](https://algotrader.ch/blog/quant-hedge-funds-lost-5-in-july-2026/)). A useful summary number is the **distance to forced deleveraging**, D = (equity − stressed margin requirement)/(σ_book,daily · NAV), measured in daily sigmas. The proposed alert is D < 10 and action at D < 5.

### An unwind leaves fingerprints in the P&L before it shows in the factors

The cleanest signature is a persistent, one-signed loss in a book that should be noise-dominated. On 7 August 2007 Pedersen's proxy **fell in 90% of ten-minute intervals**, then rose in 75% of intervals on the rebound Friday ([Pedersen 2009](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w15297/w15297.pdf)). Treated as a sign test on 39 US-session buckets, 90% negative gives z = (35.1 − 19.5)/√9.75 ≈ **5.0** (derivation). The sign statistic was therefore about 5σ even though that day's P&L was "only" about −4σ. It is a physicist's test: a sign-persistence statistic whose null is near-zero autocorrelation.

```
# Unwind detector (proposal; calibrate on in-house history and 2007, 2020, 2021, 2025, 2026)
A: z_resid = PnL_resid/σ_resid < -3  while |r_mkt/σ_mkt| < 1        # big residual loss, quiet market
B: zk = (#neg 10-min buckets - n/2)/sqrt(n/4) > 3                   # intraday sign persistence
C: conc = frac(crowded longs down) + frac(crowded shorts up) - 1 > 0.3 and PnL_C/σ_C < -2
D: crowd-mimicking basket (PB crowded longs minus most-shorted) < -2σ two days running, or < -4σ over 5d
E: λ_1 crosses the MP edge with corr(v_1,w) > 0.3, or slippage/model > 1.5 (5d),
   or a reversal-strategy proxy < -3σ (market-maker capital withdrawal)
one flag: monitor | two: intraday review + freeze on adding crowded risk | three+: playbook
```

The playbook evidence argues against blanket stop-losses on this book. Contrarian positions opened on 8 and 9 August 2007 earned **8.20% and 8.96% within five days**, unleveraged ([Khandani & Lo](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w14465/w14465.pdf)). Pedersen's toy model shows the forced seller receiving the worst average price (108 against 112) ([Pedersen 2009](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w15297/w15297.pdf)). Holding is only an option for a book that can survive its funding constraints. The real control is therefore *ex ante*: size gross and crowding so that a reference unwind (Pedersen's −25% in four days on a 6%-vol book) does not breach a limit that forces sales at the trough. In the unwind itself, the sequence is as follows (proposal). Freeze new crowded risk first. Then use liquid overlays to neutralize *unintended* exposures such as beta drift or a momentum tilt. Hold the core book while D > 10σ. Once D approaches 5σ, sell the most crowded and least liquid names early and slowly; square-root impact makes slow-and-early cheaper than fast-and-late. Re-enable signals only after slippage and spreads normalize. Resonanz's archetypes are a useful classification of unwind types: crowded-factor bleed, pod VaR cut, stat-arb liquidity shock, macro collateral squeeze and gamma-flip cascade. Its trade-off of gross-down against net-down against overlays-first is the right decision frame ([Resonanz](https://resonanzcapital.com/insights/crowding-deleveraging-a-manual-for-the-next-quant-unwind)).

## Trend books own convexity at the filter horizon and sell it below

### The straddle identity tells you what a reversal costs

CFM's Dao et al. show that trend performance is "a swap between long-term realized variance and a short-term realized variance." A simple trend model has "exactly the same exposure to the long-term variance as a portfolio of naked strangles." The convexity appears only at the filter's own timescale: "a 6 to 9 month trend system rebalanced weekly should not be expected to hedge a crash lasting a few days" ([Quantpedia summary](https://quantpedia.com/tail-protection-of-trend-following-strategies/)). Fung and Hsieh's lookback-straddle factors are the older form of the same idea ([RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v14y2001i2p313-41.html)). The algebra is short (derivation). Let the position be the cumulative return to date, s_{t−1} = Σ_{k<t} r_k. Then

P&L = Σ_t r_t Σ_{k<t} r_k = ½[R_T² − Σ_t r_t²],

which is a straddle payoff on the horizon return minus realized variance at the rebalancing frequency (the theta). Expected P&L is positive if and only if the variance ratio VR(T) = Var(R_T)/(T·Var(r)) exceeds 1. A V-shaped reversal inside the filter horizon gives R_T ≈ 0 with large Σr², so the book pays the full premium and collects no payoff. That is the shape of March 2023, when the US 2-year yield fell from 5.1% to 3.9% in three trading days ([Man Group](https://www.man.com/insights/trend-following-rolling-with-punches)), and of April 2025, when the S&P 500 fell about 13% over 3–8 April and then rose 9.5% on 9 April ([GS Marquee](https://marquee.gs.com/welcome/news/views-from-the-trading-floor/2025-in-review)).

The daily reversal metric follows directly (derivation). Let wᵢ be dollar positions and σᵢ daily vols. The perfectly aligned reversal loss is L_rev(k) = k·Σ|wᵢ|σᵢ, and the diversification ratio is DR = Σ|wᵢ|σᵢ/√(wᵀΣw). Hence **L_rev(k)/σ_P = k·DR**: a coordinated k-sigma reversal costs k·DR portfolio sigmas. A 10%-vol book (about 0.63% per day) with DR = 4 loses about 2.5% on a 1σ coordinated reversal and 7.5% on a 3σ one. That is the same order as SG CTA's −4% day in March 2023. The danger pattern is rising Σ|w|σ at constant σ_P, i.e. hidden leverage to reversal behind an unchanged VaR.

```
# Trend reversal panel (derivations; thresholds are proposals)
DR        = Σ|w_i|σ_i / sqrt(wᵀΣw);  L_rev3 = 3*Σ|w_i|σ_i           # report also in units of VaR
sat_share = risk share in markets with |s_i| > s*  (s* ≈ 0.9 in Lempérière units)
age_rw    = risk-weighted days since signal sign change
y_i       = sign(w_i,t-1) * r_i,t / σ_i,t-1                         # position-signed returns
VR_book   = Var(Σ_i y_i over τ days) / (τ·Var(daily)) , τ = 20-60   # <1 persistently = whipsaw regime
PnL by signal-age bucket {0-1m, 1-3m, 3-6m, >6m}                    # new positions bleeding = premium leg
```

Saturation matters because the forward return stops rising with signal strength. Lempérière et al. fit the next-month normalized return as a + b·s*·tanh(s/s*) with **s* ≈ 0.89**, stable for filters from 2.5 to 10 months ([Lempérière et al.](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1404.3274)). Old, saturated trends hold the most accumulated profit and the most reversal exposure while adding little expected return. Sizing that stays linear in |s| up to a clip therefore carries reversal risk with no compensation.

### Correlation collapse should be measured on position-signed returns

A 100-market book diversifies until one macro factor drives everything. When that factor trends, the book prints years like **SG Trend +27.4% in 2022**; when it reverses, every leg loses at once ([Cambridge Associates](https://www.cambridgeassociates.com/insight/does-trend-followings-recent-struggle-signal-that-the-strategy-is-structurally-broken/)). In March 2023 the book was positioned short bonds and long equities. A flight to quality hit both legs, and UBS estimated CTAs sold **$25–30bn** of equities ([Reuters via Kitco](https://www.kitco.com/news/off-the-wire/2023-03-16/trend-following-hedge-funds-whiplashed-wild-bond-trade)). In 2026 the drivers have narrowed again. CTA gains to Q3 came from long crude, long USD and short Treasuries, with **equities and bonds positively correlated and energy negatively correlated with both**. One manager describes CTA risk as concentrated and "heavily dependent on energy and rates" ([HedgeCo citing CNBC](https://hedgeco.net/news/10/2026/trend-following-hedge-funds-returned-15-7-through-the-third-quarter-outpacing-the-sp-500.html)).

The quantity to monitor is the correlation of position-signed returns yᵢ, not of raw returns. Long equities plus short bonds is a hedge under negative stock–bond correlation and a single bet under positive correlation. From the risk-weighted covariance Σ_P = diag(w)Σdiag(w), report the effective number of bets N_eff = (Σλ)²/Σλ² and the top eigenvector's share and sector loadings, which answers the question "is the book one trade?" (proposal). Thresholds should be percentiles of the book's own history. The practitioner rule of thumb of N_eff ≈ 8–20 in calm periods, falling to 3–5 in macro-dominated regimes, is unverified. The binding stress is the one that *reverses* whichever regime has been trending. In late 2026 that is an oil reversal (for example on Iran de-escalation) combined with a rates rally.

### Vol targeting overshoots after a jump, and nothing helps on the jump day

Inverse-vol sizing makes realized risk equal target × σ_true/σ̂. Suppose true vol jumps by a factor m at t = 0 and an EWMA estimator with decay λ is used. Then E[σ̂²_t] = σ₀²[1 + (m² − 1)(1 − λ^t)], and the realized/target ratio is m/√(1 + (m² − 1)(1 − λ^t)) (derivation). For a vol doubling (m = 2):

| EWMA λ (half-life) | Day 1 | Day 5 | Day 10 | Day 20 | Day 40 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.94 (~11d) | 1.84× | 1.49× | 1.30× | 1.13× | 1.03× |
| 0.97 (~23d) | 1.92× | 1.68× | 1.50× | 1.30× | 1.13× |
| 0.9885 (~60d) | 1.97× | 1.85× | 1.74× | 1.57× | 1.38× |

Slow estimators, such as the three-year windows in AQR's century study ([Hurst et al.](https://fairmodel.econ.yale.edu/ec439/hurst.pdf)), leave the book over-risked for weeks after a jump. The remedy is asymmetric estimation: size on max(σ̂_fast, σ̂_slow) and flag markets where σ̂_fast/σ̂_slow > 1.5 (proposal). Gap risk is a separate problem. The day-0 loss is position × jump, and the 5 February 2018 losses arrived on the spike day itself, when SG CTA lost about **5% in the month, its worst in 17 years** ([HedgeNordic](https://hedgenordic.com/2018/03/february-volatility-spike-beats-ctas-to-the-ground/?print=pdf)). The control for gaps is gross risk (DR and L_rev), not vol targeting. Vol scaling is also procyclical. When vol doubles and the signal falls from saturation to half, the position shrinks to about a quarter over the estimator half-life (derivation). CTA flows therefore peak in the days *after* the shock.

### Futures plumbing: margins, limits, liquidity and the exchange itself

The tail events that VaR cannot see are exchange interventions and liquidity holes. LME nickel rose **as much as 250% in two days** in March 2022, briefly passing $100,000/t. The exchange then suspended trading and cancelled all of that day's trades ([CRU](https://www.crugroup.com/en/communities/thought-leadership/2022/lme-suspends-nickel-trading/)). Cocoa hit a record in April 2024 and then fell about 20% in "record low liquidity." Aspect cut cocoa exposure from **about 5% of NAV to under 1%**, and by December 2024 open interest was the lowest since at least 2014 ([Reuters via Financial Express](https://today.thefinancialexpress.com.bd/print/how-a-hedge-fund-exodus-reshaped-global-cocoa-markets-1734889297)). CFM warns that apparent performance in illiquid CTA markets can be self-generated by inflows and reverses when flows stop ([CFM](https://www.cfm.com/the-cautious-tale-of-ctas-trading-illiquid-alternative-markets/)). The controls are all proposals. Position limits should be min(α·ADV, β·OI, γ·exchange limit). Stress should include N consecutive limit-locked days for grains and livestock. Margin-to-equity should be stress-tested at IM ×2 across all markets and ×3–5 in the stressed market. Roll and first-notice calendars should be hard-coded into checks. Track the share of risk in markets closed at the London or New York snapshot, because Asian indices gap on Western-hours news. Square-root impact gives a useful scale: exiting 20% of ADV costs about σ_d·√0.2 ≈ 0.45 daily sigmas, comparable to a full day's move (derivation).

### Herd-positioning models give direction, not dollars

Sell-side CTA models replicate generic multi-lookback trend signals on index-sized AUM and publish trigger levels and flow scenarios. In early June 2026 Goldman estimated trend followers were about **$93bn long global equities**, with **more than $100bn of selling over a month in a sustained decline** ([Investing.com via Yahoo, press-reported](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/cta-positioning-carries-lingering-selloff-110153773.html)). The same event can draw estimates differing by about 2×: August 2024 produced figures of $100bn and $170bn with undisclosed methods ([NilssonHedge](https://nilssonhedge.com/2024/08/11/cta-did-they-really-sell-130bn-worth-of-equities)). Use these models as directional indicators. Evidence of systemic overcrowding in trend is weak, with a flows–performance correlation of **−0.1** ([Man Group](https://www.man.com/insights/is-this-time-different)). Single small markets are where crowding bites. An in-house replicator calibrated to SG Trend gives three daily outputs (proposal): the herd's position percentile per market, the distance to its trigger in σ units, and the cosine similarity between the house book's risk vector and the replicator's, which measures how generic, and hence crowded, the book is.

### Drawdowns: know what "normal" looks like before cutting

For Brownian P&L, the drawdown is a reflected Brownian motion with drift −μ, whose stationary law is exponential: P(D/σ > x) = exp(−2·SR·x) (derivation). Setting this to 5% gives **x = ln20/(2·SR) ≈ 1.50/SR**, which reproduces Rej, Seager and Bouchaud's fitted 5% depth threshold. Their 5% duration threshold is **ℓ ≈ 2.14/SR² years**. At SR = 0.5 there is a 5% chance of a drawdown lasting at least 7 years ([Rej, Seager & Bouchaud](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.01457)). At SG Trend's roughly 13.6% annual σ ([Man Group](https://www.man.com/insights/trend-following-rolling-with-punches)), the May 2024–May 2025 drawdown of **−20.4%**, the second-largest since 2000 ([Cambridge Associates](https://www.cambridgeassociates.com/insight/does-trend-followings-recent-struggle-signal-that-the-strategy-is-structurally-broken/)), is about 1.5σ. That sits inside the 5% envelope for any SR up to about 1. Man's simulations find that 20% drawdown stop rules on SR-0.6 trend strategies **cost about 2.2% per year** against holding ([Man Group](https://www.man.com/insights/is-this-time-different)). Three corrections are needed (derivation/proposal). Compute thresholds at SR̂ − 1·SE, since SR uncertainty dominates. Replace σ with the Newey–West long-run σ, because positive autocorrelation inflates depth thresholds by (1 + 2Σρ_k). Measure depth on vol-normalized cumulative P&L so that a volatility-regime drawdown can be told apart from a loss of edge. RSB stress that the test cannot distinguish bad luck from an overestimated Sharpe or a misspecified model. It is a review trigger, not a stop.

## Every estimator fails somewhere, so monitor the failure as well as the number

**Covariance.** The best-documented industrial template is Barra USE4. It uses a factor-vol half-life of 84 days, a correlation half-life of 504, Newey–West lags of 5 and 2, and a volatility-regime adjustment (VRA). The VRA scales the whole covariance by an EWMA (half-life 42) of the squared cross-sectional bias statistic B_t² = mean_k(f_kt/σ_kt)², so all vols move together without changing correlations ([MSCI USE4](https://www.top1000funds.com/wp-content/uploads/2011/09/USE4_Methodology_Notes_August_2011.pdf)). Two noise effects matter for hedged books. First, EWMA with half-life h has an effective sample of about 2.885h, so q = N/N_eff must stay small (derivation). Second, optimized portfolios are biased. Pafka and Kondor show that at T/n = 2 a minimum-variance portfolio's in-sample risk is **0.71** of truth and its out-of-sample risk **1.43**, and that RMT filtering cuts the excess from more than 40% to about 10% ([Pafka & Kondor](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/cond-mat/0305475)). USE4 finds the lowest-vol eigenportfolios under-predicted by about **40%** ([MSCI USE4](https://www.top1000funds.com/wp-content/uploads/2011/09/USE4_Methodology_Notes_August_2011.pdf)). A market-neutral spread is exactly such a portfolio, so its bias statistic is the most sensitive health test available. Bun, Bouchaud and Potters' rotationally invariant estimator is the current best cleaner ([arXiv:1610.08104](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.08104)). A practical CTA design keeps q ≈ 0.1 with N ≈ 100–150 futures and a 504-day correlation half-life. A 2,000-stock book needs a factor model.

**Asynchrony.** Close-to-close returns across time zones bias correlations toward zero ([Burns via R-bloggers](https://www.r-bloggers.com/2011/11/asynchrony-in-market-data/)). If information arrives uniformly, the measured same-day correlation is ρ·f, where f is the overlap fraction of the two return windows. For Tokyo against New York, f ≈ 0.375, so the measured correlation is about 40% of the true value (derivation). A cross-regional hedge or a Nikkei-versus-ES trend position then looks more diversified than it is. The fixes, in order of preference, are a common snapshot time (clean for futures), Bartlett-weighted lag-1 cross-covariances, and Dimson or Scholes–Williams betas.

**VaR and ES.** Filtered historical simulation is the right daily default. Standardize each factor by its own lagged EWMA vol on the same historical date across factors, rescale by today's vol, and reprice. This keeps the empirical copula and fat tails while reacting to the regime. Its blind spot is correlation: only marginal vols are rescaled. Complement it with unfiltered crisis-day replays, or covariance-scale the scenarios with Σ_T^{1/2}Σ_t^{−1/2}. Under normality ES₉₇.₅ = 2.338σ ≈ VaR₉₉ = 2.326σ (derivation), so the gap between them is a direct fat-tail gauge. Square-root-of-time scaling is wrong for both books, in opposite directions. Variance scales as hσ²[1 + 2Σ(1 − k/h)ρ_k]. Trend P&L is positively autocorrelated in trends, and inside a horizon the signal and vol rules change positions. Reversal-based stat-arb P&L is negatively autocorrelated. Ten-day risk should come from path simulation that re-runs the position rules. EVT (POT/GPD on FHS residuals) belongs in 99%+ calibration and stress, not in the daily number, because parameters are unstable with about 50 exceedances.

**Backtesting power.** This is the most underappreciated failure. At 99% and T = 250, a model with true **98% coverage passes Basel's 5-exception threshold 43.9% of the time**, and a 97%-coverage model passes it 12.8% of the time ([BCBS 1996](https://www.bis.org/publications/199601-standards-supervisory-framework-use-backtesting-conjunction-internal-models-approach-market-risk-capital.pdf)). With 2.5 expected exceptions, the Christoffersen clustering test has almost nothing to work with. The bias statistic B = std(PnL_t/σ̂_{t−1}) has standard error √((κ − 1)/(4T)), which gives a 95% band of **[0.91, 1.09] at κ = 3 and [0.86, 1.14] at κ = 6** for T = 250 (derivation). It therefore resolves 9–14% vol misspecification, far finer than exception counts. Kurtosis widens the bands: at κ = 5 and T = 120, only 86% of bias statistics fall inside the nominal 95% band ([MSCI USE4](https://www.top1000funds.com/wp-content/uploads/2011/09/USE4_Methodology_Notes_August_2011.pdf)). For ES, Acerbi and Szekely's Z2 = Σ_t X_t·1{X_t < −VaR_t}/(T·α·ES_t) + 1 needs only the daily VaR and ES. Its critical values are stable across distributions, **−0.7 (5%) and −1.8 (0.01%)**, matching the traffic-light levels. Its power is comparable to or better than the exception test ([Acerbi & Szekely](https://www.msci.com/documents/1296102/1636401/risk1214msci.pdf)). These are the only sourced alarm thresholds in this report. Rank challenger models with QLIKE, use the bias statistic for absolute calibration, and keep Kupiec counts as a summary.

```
# Model-health block (proposal assembling sourced tests)
b = HPL_t / σ̂_{t-1}
B60, B250 = std(b[-60:]), std(b[-250:]); band = 1 ± 1.96*sqrt((kurt(b)-1)/(4*250))
x95, x975, x99 = exception counts; Kupiec p-values; Basel zone at 99%
Z2 over 250d: yellow < -0.7, red < -1.8
Berkowitz LR on Φ^{-1}(PIT); QLIKE(production) vs QLIKE(challengers)
also by book, factor group and eigenportfolio; HPL-RTPL (missing factors, roll, FX, snapshot)
alert: B outside band > 20 consecutive days, any p < 0.01, or Z2 yellow
```

**Stress testing.** Breuer et al. show that hand-picked scenarios create "an illusion of safety." A plausible-looking 5.42σ macro scenario barely moved a portfolio whose worst case at the same Mahalanobis radius was catastrophic ([Breuer et al.](https://www.ijcb.org/sites/default/files/journal/v5n3/ijcb-v5n3-how-find-plausible-severe-and-useful-stress-scenarios.pdf)). For a linear book the worst case has a closed form (derivation). Within the ellipsoid of radius k it is **r* = −kΣw/√(wᵀΣw), with loss kσ_p**. The worst direction is the vector of each factor's covariance with the portfolio. The reverse stress for a target loss L sits at Mahalanobis distance L/σ_p, a number worth reporting daily as "sigmas to lose L". Do not choose k from χ² quantiles: in 50 dimensions the 99% radius is √76.2 ≈ 8.7 (derivation). Calibrate k to the Mahalanobis distances of real episodes instead. Add second-round effects explicitly by applying square-root impact to the crowd's estimated liquidation and iterating once.

**Attribution.** Euler contributions wᵢ(Σw)ᵢ/σ_p add up to fund vol. ES contributions from roughly 25 tail scenarios are noisy, so smooth them or show them next to the vol contributions. Daily change attribution should use the symmetric (Shapley) split, Position = ½[(w₁ᵀΣ₀w₁ − w₀ᵀΣ₀w₀) + (w₁ᵀΣ₁w₁ − w₀ᵀΣ₁w₀)], with the market term subdivided into vol, correlation and VRA-scalar parts. For HS/FHS, add a "window roll" term, which often explains unexplained VaR jumps (derivation).

## Early warnings come in three layers, and few thresholds are validated

No single indicator predicts a quant equity unwind. The 2025–2026 episodes occurred in calm markets, so the dashboard has to stack three layers. The first is positioning and crowding. The second is market-implied stress. The third is systematic-flow estimates. Funding and liquidity sit above them as the escalation layer. Published predictive evidence exists only for absorption-type and factor-comovement measures, and even those come without validated cut-offs. ## The books meet in momentum, beta, rates and above all the cash pool

Unconditionally the two books are close to uncorrelated. No sourced crisis-conditional correlation between EMN and CTA indices was found, so no number should be assumed. The interaction is regime-dependent. In slow persistent bear markets (2008, 2022) trend makes money and diversification works. In a quant-specific crowding unwind (August 2007) the stat-arb book loses while trend is unaffected. In a V-shaped policy reversal the outcome varies. In April 2025 the books *diverged*: HFRX Equity Market Neutral returned **+0.17%** while HFRX Macro Systematic/CTA lost **4.49%** ([HFR](https://www.hfr.com/?p=39180)). In October 2026 they diverged the other way: HFRI Systematic Diversified/CTA returned **−0.1%** ([Hedgeweek](https://hedgeweek.com/news/hedge-funds-decline-1-4-in-october-as-technology-selloff-hits-performance)) while PivotalPath's EMN index returned **−2.48%** ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/even-market-neutral-funds-couldnt-escape-ai-slump-october)). In a funding crisis (March 2020) both books face margin calls at once.

Five channels can align the books (inference, ranked). The first is **momentum at both ends**: residual momentum tilts in the equity book and time-series momentum in the futures book both lose in sharp rebounds. The second is **equity beta**: a CTA long equity indices after a rally plus a small residual long beta in the stat-arb book makes the fund net long into a correction, so the dashboard needs one combined equity-beta number. The third is **rates**: style and sector tilts give the equity book duration exposure, and the CTA holds large bond positions. The fourth is **shared funding**, the strongest crisis link even when P&L correlation is low. The fifth is **crowding**. The size of the diversification illusion is easy to compute (derivation). Two equal-risk books at ρ = 0 give σ_fund = σ_book/√2. At ρ = 0.5, σ_fund = 0.87σ_book, which is **22% more fund vol** for the same positions. Budgeting at a stressed correlation of +0.3 to +0.5, reporting the diversification benefit at both full-sample and stressed ρ, and recomputing cross-book correlation on days with |fund return| > 2σ are all proposals that turn this from an assumption into a monitored quantity. Stress limits should be set on the joint scenario, with a separate cap on the *sum* of standalone stress losses, so that no diversification is assumed in the tail.

The cash link has a precise calibration. From 31 January to 20 March 2020, euro-area CCPs collected about **€30bn of extra initial margin (+32%)**, and clearing members passed on **€23bn to clients (+55%)**. Investment funds were the largest client group hit. Margin stayed elevated for the rest of 2020 ([ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/pub/financial-stability/fsr/focus/2021/html/ecb.fsrbox202111_09~4f2668eed6.de.html)). The gap between +55% and +32% means FCM add-ons are themselves procyclical. The equity book's prime-broker margin is discretionary and can be repriced overnight. The futures book's variation margin is cash every day, so a −3σ trend day creates same-day calls just as the PB raises house margin. Index-future hedges held at an FCM do not net against swaps at the PB, so a hedged position consumes margin on both legs. All of this argues for one fund-level cash ladder (proposal):

```
stressed_need = IM_CCP(replayed at Mar-2020 / 2022 vols) * FCM_addon
              + PB_margin * (1.25 to 1.5)             # house-margin step-up, concentration add-ons
              + VM_outflow(1-5d, at -3σ CTA day)
coverage      = unencumbered_cash / stressed_need     # attention < 2, action < 1.5
M/E_stressed  = Σ IM_stressed / NAV                   # limit on the stressed, not the current, value
per-PB exposure measured as excess collateral at risk; PB CDS as early warning
```

Archegos is the governance lesson, not a metrics lesson. Credit Suisse's potential exposure was **$530m against a $20m limit**, on data up to six weeks stale. The risks "were identified and were conspicuous," yet staff "failed at multiple junctures to take decisive and urgent action" ([Axios on the Paul Weiss report](https://www.axios.com/2021/07/30/credit-suisse-archegos-risk)). The limit tree (fund → book → sector → instrument, with soft and hard levels) is standard. What matters is that hard breaches force action within a stated horizon, and that waivers are time-boxed, minuted and shown with their age on the dashboard.

Drawdown limits need vol units. Man's simulation shows that a manager with 10% vol and Sharpe 0.5 has a **97.1% chance of hitting −10% within ten years**, 43.0% of hitting −20% and 9.9% of hitting −30% ([Man Institute](https://www.man.com/insights/youre-fired)). Pod platforms reportedly cut capital at about −5% and stop out at −7% to −7.5% ([Hedgeweek](https://hedgeweek.com/news/bluecrest-and-millennium-closed-pods-amid-market-turmoil); [eFinancialCareers citing WSJ](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/millennium-hedge-fund-jobs)). Those figures are secondhand and not confirmed as firm rules. A −7.5% stop is −1.5σ on a 5%-vol stat-arb book but only −0.5σ on a 15%-vol trend book, where it would trigger far too often. The proposed structure is book-level review at −1σ annual and stepwise capital cuts at −1.5σ to −2σ, restored by rule, with fund-level escalation at RSB p < 5%. There is one more interaction to model explicitly. The trend book's internal vol scaling, a fund-level vol target and drawdown cuts all de-gross in the same direction during a reversal. Their half-lives should be coordinated, and the combined de-leveraging path, including impact on the equity book's less liquid names, should be simulated.

The escalation ladder (proposal) works as follows. Level 0 is an automatic alert to the PM. Level 1 is a soft breach, resolved and logged the same day. Level 2 is a hard breach, or a soft breach unresolved after one to two days: the CRO and CIO are informed and the reduction executes within a set horizon. Level 3 covers a fund drawdown trigger, a cash-coverage breach, a counterparty event or three or more unwind flags: a same-day risk committee with the ManCo notified. Risk reports to the CRO, not the CIO. A data-quality gate withholds or flags the morning pack when reconciliation breaks exceed a threshold, because stale data combined with waivers is where failures concentrate. For a UK AIFM, AIFMD defines leverage as "any method by which the AIFM increases the exposure of an AIF" ([Linklaters](https://www.linklaters.com/insights/publications/aifmd/leverage)). A futures-heavy book's gross leverage will look very large at moderate risk, so investor and regulatory reporting should pair it with risk-based measures. The Annex IV thresholds and the 2026 UK regime status were not re-verified.

## Conclusion

The common thread is that both books' worst days are *model-structure* failures rather than tail draws, and the structure can be measured in advance. For stat-arb the missing piece is a correlation term, ρ̄ across residuals, that the risk model assumes is zero. For trend it is a time-scale mismatch: reversals faster than the filter turn a long-convexity strategy into a short one. For the fund it is a liquidity coupling, a shared collateral pool whose demands rise exactly when both books lose. Each failure has a cheap daily observable: the residual eigenvector's alignment with book weights, DR·k for coordinated reversals, the σ̂_fast/σ̂_slow ratio, and the cash coverage of stressed margin. Each has a pre-committed response that acts on *state* rather than on realized P&L. That is where the evidence points, given that holders are paid in liquidity events and drawdown stops are expensive.

Statistical power is the second, less obvious implication. Most dashboards lead with the measure least able to detect a broken model, the Kupiec count, while the bias statistic, Z2 and the intraday sign test resolve misspecification in weeks rather than years. The honest gap is calibration. Apart from Z2, no threshold here has published hit rates. The most valuable in-house project is to back-test the unwind flags, crowding z-scores and reversal metrics on the fund's own history and on the 2007, 2020, 2021, 2025 and 2026 episodes, and then replace the proposed numbers with measured ones.
